## Day 0: Delhi to Agra (Nov 28)

### The big day

Nervous? Absolutely. Excited? You bet. Woke up early to clock-in early, and clocked-out early around 5 PM. Did my packing throught he lunch break. Got my bagpack ready and mounted, strapped and secured my tank bag, gave Murphy a last minute check-up at 3611 kms, wore my riding gear (just the jacket tbh), and hit the road at 5:45 PM. Tank filled with oil, lungs filled with air, ears filled with my favourite music, heart filled with excitement and mind fueled by adrenaline, I was ready to go.

Long ago, as I'd decided to take this journey as a traveller, not a tourist, I'd also made a promise to myself, that the only use of internet on this trip would be to do two things, and I'm glad I stuck to it:
1. Navigation
2. Booking accomodation

### Warming up on chilly evenings

First leg of my journey, was a simple warm-up leg of around 200 kms, from my home to Agra. It was a Friday evening, and the traffic was as expected, terrible. Not all was bad though, it was a straightforward highway road on NH 44, and I was able to maintain a good speed throughout, sometimes reaching upto 155 kmph on straight stretches, increasing my confidence. Passed by Palwal, Vrindavan, Mathura and reached Tajganj around 9:00 PM.

Honestly? The first thing that surprised me was that Agra had a good functional underground metro system. Near the Taj Mahal metro station, I stopped by the pani-puri wala for some quick snacks and opened up makemytrip to book a hostel for the night. Checked in, bought the ticket to see the Taj Mahal tomorrow, did a quick face wash and hit the bed, ready to wake up early tomorrow morning to visit the Taj Mahal.

## Day 1: Agra to Nagpur (Nov 29)

### Taj Mahal

Quick piece of trivia for you: The gates for Taj Mahal don't have a fixed time of opening or closing. It depends on the time of sunrise and sunset. They open half an hour prior to sunrise and close half an hour before sunset. Sinrise that day was at 6:48 AM, gates opened at 6:18 AM. I woke up at 5, did a quick was in *freezing water*, got ready, packed my stuff and was at the east gate of Taj Mahal by 5:40 AM.

To the surprise of no one, there was already a queue formed outside the gate. For probably the first time in my life, I was happy to be in an Indian-only queue. Dude, the foreigners' queue was like 5 times longer than hours. Got myself a water bottle and some shoe covers (can't go into the tomb without them now-a-days) stading in the queue. After waiting for more than half an hour, and a quick yet thorough security check, I was in. And boy, was it worth it.

![Taj Mahal Solo Shot](/v1768567214/taj-mahal-solo-shot_vcddlr.jpg)

After a quick look around, and some mesmerizing shots, I returned back to my hostel by 7:30. Had to spend about half hour debugging a production issue on a Saturday morning (perks of being the on-call engineer) before I could get breakfast in the hostel's rooftop cafe. The views were alright, but I had a ticking timer in my head. I had to reach Hyderabad in the next 2 days and according to ChatGPT, the route I was planning was pretty close to maniacal, 1300+ kms is no joke, especially on Indian roads. Had a light breakfast, drank 3 cups of coffee, packed my bags and headed downstairs to my bike.

### My longest ride

Both the bags strapped on, helmet on my arm, I checked the route I was going to take to Hyderabad. Seoni and Nagpur were the two potential stops I could stay for the night before heading onwards. Indecisiveness got the better of me, and I put Nagpur as my destination in the navigation, took a deep breath and put on my helmet. It was already around 9 and I decided that if I get exhausted or tired, I would stop at Seoni, take a road-side hotel there somewhere along the highway and call it a day.

Nagpur was more than 800 kms away (~15.5 hours). Starting at 9 from Agra would've meant it would be almost 9 PM by the time I would see the first "Nagpur up ahead" Signs on the highways. Not me. Cruising at an average speed of 135 kmph on Murphy, dodging trucks, tankers, jeeps, buses, cars, bullock-carts and other two wheelers, I croosed UP, crossing Rajasthan via Dholpur and continuing my journey southwards till Morena, entering into Madhya Pradesh. Taking the Gwalior bypass via NH 44, I found myself on the outskirts of Jhansi by noon. By now, the sun was at its peak, so I got off at a dhaba for some cold drink to replenish my electrolytes. Persevering, I spent close to three hours passing through the small towns of Babina, Talbehat, Lalitpur and Bandri crossing Sagar around 3.

It was almost 6 hours on the road for now, and the majority of riders will tell you that's more than enough for a day, and that you should rest. Ideally. But me? Oh I was faaaaaaar from it. I did stop, but for a quick lunch at a roadside dhaba for 30 mins. I resumed my journey at 3:30, crossing Deori and making my way to Narsinghpur and then reaching Seoni by 6 PM. I was dead tired by now, the sunset was clearly visible, and I had finally understood why it was called the Deccan "Plateau" in CBSE geography textbooks. You might've read about it, I LIVED IT.

## Day 2: Nagpur to Hyderabad (Nov 30)

### Heading to the starting line

I call Hyderabad my "starting line" because the way I had this trip planned, I primarily wanted to explore South India as I haven't been there properly. And well, my rudimentary doc started from Hyderabad. The distance to Hyderabad was not that much, coming in just shy of 500 kms, and 9 hours, so I was not *as worried as yesterday*. My alarm woke me up at 6 AM, I packed my stuff up, headed downstairs, strapped my luggage, checked-out, and was on my way before 7 AM, before sunrise.

Murphy was excited, I was well rested (dreamless sleep), and there was only one goal in my mind. Push my limits even further. Leaving at 7 in the morning with an estimated drive time of 9 hours meant that I'd reach Hyderabad *just in time* to see Chaarminar before sunrise. But you already know me. Driving recklessly simply comes naturally for some reason. I checked-in the hostel, and immediately took Murphy out again. This time, not for a drive, but to get its servicing done. It was a Sunday, and I checked at three different Royal Enfied Service Centres if they'll be able to service it till today evening. No luck. Came back to the hostel around 2:30 PM, took a long hot shower and headed outside by 3:45 PM, this time to eat since I had not eaten properly since yesterday's lunch. Yeah, I was in a bad condition.

### Hotel Shadab

A colleague of mine from work, Srithan, is originally from Hyderabad. I called him up and asked for restaurant recommendations. I'm in Hyderabad, ain't no way I'm skipping the legendary Biryani. He recommended me Hotel Shadab and warned me that it'll be bsy and the wait times can range from 10 mins to hours. Didn't scare me even a little. See, the place he recommended was on the way to Chaarminar and was actually waking distance from it. And if there's one thing I was planning to do on this trip? Get authentic experiences, not curated ones. For anyone curious, here's what he recommended me to try there:

- Shadab Special Chicken Biryani (Ghosh)
- Tandoori Chicken
- Mutton Paaya Soup (Mutton Legs)
- Thumbs Up (glass one)
- Mirchi ka salan + Lemons & Onions
- Irani Chai + Osmania Biscuit

Yeah. It's a lot. And it was a GOOOOOD thing I was hungry asf. I reached there, got in line, and waited for around 20 minutes. The thing is, I was by myself. And the tables over there seat exactly 4. It was peak time and there was no way I was getting a table all to myself. Fortunately, and thanks to my great luck, I was seated with three people a couple years older than me and believe me when I tell you this, we had a blast. Two guys and a girl. All three were in Hyderabad for a trip as well. Two were from IISc Bangalore (Vaishnavi and Alvin Joshua) and one was from Gujrat, working in Vijaywada (Rutvik).

Rutvik and I bonded particularly well since we shared the same travel gene and the craze for adventure. We exchanged travel stories, and I told them about my 23 Before 23 plan. They were amazed, and we exchanged contact numbers, to keep in touch in case any of us were planning a trip sometime soon, New Year's for example.

We ~~over~~ate and then had the Irani Chai and Osmania Biscuit combo there itself. After settling the bill, I told them I'll be heading on to Chaarminar to get some shots in before sunset. Rutvik said, and I quote, `"Toh chalo saath mein, hum bhi udhar hi jaa rhe h"`
